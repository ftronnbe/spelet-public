Privacy policy

Effective from 28 October 2026.

Deep Regrats is a game for iPhone. This policy says what the game stores, what it sends, to whom and why, and what you can do about it. The short version: your game is saved on your phone, with a copy in your own iCloud. Apple handles Game Center and purchases. Google shows the ads. We do not run any server that receives your game data, we do not have accounts, and we do not sell anything about you.

Who we are

Deep Regrats is made by Flipware in Sweden. Flipware is the controller for the personal data described here. Questions about your data go to team.app.consortium{at}gmail{dot}com (replace {at} with @ and {dot} with a full stop).

What the game stores on your phone

  • Your save: your hero (class, looks, level, skills, gear and inventory), progress through the floors, the Town Fund, quests, achievements, statistics such as play time and kills, and the cat (his name and his hats).
  • Names you choose: the hero's name and the cat's name are typed by you and stored in the save. They are never sent anywhere.
  • Settings: music, sound, effects quality, and small notes the game keeps for itself, such as your daily streak, whether it has already asked about notifications, and when it last showed an ad (so it can keep to at most one every five minutes).
  • Remove Ads: whether you own it. The game asks Apple's StoreKit on your phone; the answer is not stored by us anywhere else.

This stays in the game's own storage on your phone. A copy of your characters is also kept in your own iCloud (iCloud key-value storage, under Apple's terms), so a new phone can pick them up. It belongs to your Apple ID and we cannot see it. To turn it off: iOS Settings → your name → iCloud → Deep Regrats (or Show All Apps). Like other app data it may be included in your phone's backups (iCloud Backup or a computer backup), under Apple's terms. Deleting the game deletes it. The game does not use your location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone, and it asks for none of them.

What is sent, and to whom

  • Apple: Game Center (achievements and the Deepest Floor leaderboard), if you are signed in, and in-app purchases. See Game Center and purchases.
  • Google (AdMob): what is needed to show ads. See Advertising.
  • Our bug-report backend: only from beta builds (TestFlight), and only when you send a report yourself. App Store builds have no bug reporter. See Bug reports.

Nothing else. There is no analytics or tracking SDK in the game apart from Google's advertising SDK, and the game has no server of its own that receives your game data.

Advertising

The game is free and pays for itself with a few ads, shown by Google AdMob (Google Ireland Limited and Google LLC). There are two kinds:

  • An optional revive: when your hero dies you may choose to watch a short rewarded ad to come back. Only if you tap it.
  • One interstitial after a boss floor: when the portal after a boss floor takes you back to town. Never in the first five minutes of a session, and never more than one every five minutes. Remove Ads switches these off.

What Google collects

To choose, show and count an ad and to prevent fraud, Google's SDK in the game collects:

  • your IP address, from which Google works out an approximate location (country or city level);
  • information about your device and the app: model, iOS version, language, app version, and that the ad was shown in Deep Regrats;
  • how you interact with the ads: that an ad loaded, was shown, watched to the end or tapped;
  • technical crash and performance data about the ad SDK itself;
  • the advertising identifier (IDFA), only if you allow tracking when iOS asks.

Google uses this to deliver and measure ads and, only where you have agreed, to personalise them. Google processes the data partly for us and partly as an independent controller under its own policy: policies.google.com/privacy and how Google uses information from apps that use its services. We never receive data that identifies you; AdMob only shows us totals.

Your choices

  • The consent message (EEA, UK and Switzerland): the first time you are back in town, the game shows Google's consent message. It asks whether Google may use your data for personalised ads and measurement. You can say no; the game works the same. Personalised ads are only shown if you agree. To change your answer later: pause menu → REMOVE ADS → PRIVACY CHOICES.
  • The tracking prompt: once, at the same calm moment, iOS asks whether the game may track you ("Lets Google AdMob use this device's advertising identifier to show you more relevant ads. Declining is fine: the game stays the same."). Google may first show a short screen of its own that explains the question. If you decline, the advertising identifier is not shared and ads are not personalised with it. You can change this any time: iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking, or turn off "Allow Apps to Request to Track" altogether.
  • Reset or limit the advertising identifier: on recent iOS versions the identifier is all zeros unless you allow tracking. Turning tracking off for the game in the settings above is the way to stop it.
  • No personalisation: if you decline both, you still see ads, but they are not personalised. They may be based on context, such as the game and your approximate location.
  • No interstitials: buy Remove Ads (below). The optional revive ad remains, because you choose it.

Remove Ads, purchases and refunds

  • Remove Ads is a one-time purchase (29 kr in Sweden; Apple sets the matching price elsewhere) that removes the ads after boss floors for good. It is the game's only purchase.
  • Payment is handled entirely by Apple through the App Store. We never see your card details, name or Apple ID. The game only learns from Apple's StoreKit, on your phone, whether you own Remove Ads.
  • Restoring: on a new or reset phone, pause menu → REMOVE ADS → RESTORE PURCHASES.
  • Refunds are handled by Apple, not by us: request one at reportaproblem.apple.com. Apple's terms decide.

Game Center

If you are signed in to Game Center on your iPhone, the game reports the achievements you unlock and the deepest floor you reach to Apple. Others may see your Game Center nickname and score on the Deepest Floor leaderboard, according to your Game Center settings. This is handled by Apple under Apple's privacy policy; we only see the leaderboard like any player. If you are not signed in, the game keeps achievements on your phone and sends nothing.

The daily reminder

After you have seen the dungeon, the game asks once whether it may send notifications. If you allow it, it schedules one reminder a day, on your phone, at the time of day you last played ("The dungeon misses you."). It is a local notification: nothing goes through a server and nothing is sent to us. Turn it off in iOS Settings → Notifications → Deep Regrats.

Bug reports (beta builds only)

Beta versions of the game, installed through TestFlight, have a built-in bug reporter. App Store versions do not: the game checks with Apple which kind of installation it is and switches the reporter off. Installs made by App Review during the review of the game count as beta builds. In a beta, pressing a volume button opens a report form over the game, and shaking the phone opens the list of reports. If, and only if, you press send, this goes to our bug-report backend (a database and server function hosted by Supabase):

  • what you write (title, description, comments) and the name you pick as reporter;
  • a screenshot of the game screen at that moment, with anything you drew on it;
  • technical context: app version and build number, iOS version, device model, language and region setting, and where you were in the game (screen, floor, class, level, position and play time this session).

It is used only to find and fix the problem. Do not write personal information in a report.

Crash reports

Not yet: the game sends no crash reports of its own. If you have turned on "Share With App Developers" in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements, Apple may pass anonymous crash logs to us under Apple's terms. You can turn that off there.

Legal bases (GDPR)

  • Consent (Article 6(1)(a)): personalised ads, ad measurement that needs consent, and the advertising identifier. You can withdraw it any time as described above, without losing anything in the game.
  • Legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f)): showing non-personalised ads to keep the game free, including the IP address and device information Google needs to deliver an ad and stop fraud; and bug reports from beta builds, to fix the game. You can object (see your rights).
  • Contract (Article 6(1)(b)): delivering Remove Ads once you have bought it.

How long

  • On your phone: until you delete the game.
  • Ad data at Google: as set out in Google's policies; we do not hold it.
  • Bug reports: while the issue is being handled, and deleted at the latest 12 months after the report is closed. Ask and we delete yours sooner.

Your rights

  • Access, correction and deletion: ask us at the address below. Most of your data never reaches us: what is on your phone, you delete by deleting the game. For bug reports you sent, we can find and delete them if you tell us roughly when and what you wrote.
  • Withdraw consent: pause menu → REMOVE ADS → PRIVACY CHOICES for Google's consent message, and iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking for the advertising identifier.
  • Object to processing based on legitimate interest, and ask for restriction or a copy of your data (portability).
  • Ad data: we cannot identify you in Google's ad data. Google's own tools at myadcenter.google.com and the settings above are the way to control it.
  • Complain: you can always turn to the Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection (IMY), imy.se, or to the data protection authority where you live.

Children

Deep Regrats is not directed at children under 13. Ads are not shown or targeted differently for children, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from children. If you believe a child has sent us something, such as a bug report, contact us and we will delete it.

Transfers outside the EU

Google may process ad data in the USA and elsewhere; Google relies on the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and the EU's standard contractual clauses. Supabase, which hosts the beta bug reports, processes them for us under a data processing agreement and the EU's standard contractual clauses where data leaves the EU. Apple handles Game Center and purchases under its own terms.

Changes

If the game starts doing something new with data, this policy is updated first and the date at the top changes. If a change matters to you, we will say so in the game or in the update notes before it applies.
